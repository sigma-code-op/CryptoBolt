How to read it

What each Bitcoin number tells you

Each number in the panel answers a different question. Here is what to take from them when you are looking at Bitcoin specifically.

Price and 24h change

The last traded BTC/USDT price on Binance spot and the move over the last rolling 24 hours. A large 24h change on its own says little; compare it with the 24h range and volume before reading anything into it.

24h high and low

The extremes of the rolling day. When price sits near the high with falling volume, the move is running on less participation; near the low with rising volume, sellers are still active. Neither is a signal on its own, it is context for where the market has already been.

24h volume

Quote volume in dollars traded on the BTC/USDT spot pair. Bitcoin’s volume is far deeper than any altcoin’s, which is why slippage on market orders is usually small compared with smaller coins.

Funding rate

The payment between longs and shorts on the BTCUSDT perpetual future, shown per funding interval (typically every eight hours on Binance). Positive means longs pay shorts, which tends to happen when the market leans long. Read the funding rate guide for how to use it.

Open interest

The total value of open BTC perpetual contracts. Rising open interest alongside a price move means new positions are being opened; falling open interest during a move usually means positions are being closed instead.

Context

What actually moves the Bitcoin price

BTC trades around the clock across global spot and futures venues, so a move rarely has a single cause. These are the four drivers traders weigh most often.

The halving cycle

Roughly every 210,000 blocks (about four years) the reward paid to miners is cut in half. The April 2024 halving reduced it from 6.25 to 3.125 BTC per block. Many traders track the cycle because new supply slows while demand does not, but there have only been four halvings, and the event is widely anticipated, so treat it as background rather than a timer.

Spot ETF flows

US-listed spot Bitcoin ETFs began trading in January 2024. Their daily creations and redemptions show whether fund buyers are adding or removing BTC. Sustained net inflows can absorb supply and heavy outflows can add selling pressure, but issuers publish the figures after the fact, so flows describe what already happened.

Futures positioning

Most Bitcoin volume is in derivatives. When the funding rate is strongly positive, longs are paying shorts and the market is crowded one way. A modest dip can then trigger a liquidation cascade that pushes price much further than the news justifies.

Macro and liquidity

Bitcoin often trades as a liquidity-sensitive asset, reacting to interest-rate expectations, the US dollar and appetite for risk in equities. That link changes over time and can break during crypto-specific events, so use it as one input among several.

Key idea: Before blaming news for a sudden BTC move, check the funding rate and open interest. A fast move with no headline is often leveraged positions being closed, not new information.

Trading Bitcoin without guessing the risk

Bitcoin is the least volatile of the major coins, but leverage changes that quickly. As an illustration only: a long opened at $60,000 with 10x isolated leverage is liquidated roughly 9.6% lower, near $54,200, once a small maintenance margin is included. At 25x the same trade is liquidated after a drop of only about 3.6%, near $57,800. Those are worked examples, not forecasts or current prices, and the exact figure depends on the exchange’s maintenance-margin tier.

FAQ

Bitcoin questions traders ask

Where does the live Bitcoin price on this page come from?

It comes from Binance’s public market-data endpoints, requested directly by your browser and refreshed every ten seconds. The price shown is the BTC/USDT spot price, so it can differ slightly from other exchanges or from a US-dollar quote.

Why is Bitcoin quoted in USDT instead of USD?

Most of the deepest Bitcoin liquidity on Binance is against USDT, a stablecoin designed to hold one US dollar. The BTC/USDT price is therefore a close stand-in for the dollar price, with small differences when USDT trades slightly above or below its peg.

How often does the Bitcoin halving happen, and when is the next one?

The block reward halves every 210,000 blocks, which works out to about every four years. The last one was in April 2024, cutting the reward to 3.125 BTC. The next is expected around 2028, depending on actual block times.

Why does Bitcoin move on weekends when stock markets are closed?

Bitcoin trades 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Weekend liquidity is often thinner, so a single large order or a wave of liquidations can move price more than the same order would on a weekday.

What does Bitcoin dominance mean?

Bitcoin dominance is BTC’s share of the total crypto market capitalisation. Rising dominance often means money is concentrating in Bitcoin; falling dominance often accompanies altcoin rallies. It is a ratio, so it can rise while BTC itself falls if altcoins fall faster.
