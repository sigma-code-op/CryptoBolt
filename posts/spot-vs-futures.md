Both show up as a price on a chart, which makes it easy to gloss over how differently they actually work underneath.

## Ownership

A spot trade means you hold the asset itself — buy 0.1 BTC and you own 0.1 BTC, full stop. A perpetual futures contract is a derivative: you're holding a position that tracks BTC's price without ever owning the coin. That distinction is why spot has no liquidation risk and futures does.

## Leverage and liquidation

Futures let you open a position larger than your posted margin — 5x, 10x, sometimes more. That amplifies gains and losses equally, and if the market moves far enough against you, the exchange closes the position automatically at the liquidation price to prevent your balance from going negative. ## Funding rate

Perpetuals never expire, so exchanges use a periodic funding payment between longs and shorts to keep the contract price anchored near spot. When funding is positive, longs pay shorts, which usually means positioning is leaning long — one more piece of context the AI research pass folds in.

Neither is inherently better. Spot is simpler and caps your downside at zero; futures adds leverage and funding dynamics on top. Know which one you're actually looking at before you size a trade.
