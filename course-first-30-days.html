    Your first 30 days: a safe practice plan

    By the end of this lesson: Follow a structured month of paper trading, judge your results honestly, and know exactly when and how to approach real money.

    You have the ideas. Now you need reps. This final lesson turns everything into a 30-day plan you can follow with virtual money, and a clear test for when (and whether) to go further.

    The 30-day plan

    WhenFocusWhat to do
    Days 1 to 7Watch and learn the toolsNo trades yet. Open the terminal daily, read the daily and 1-hour charts of 2 pairs, mark trend and support/resistance zones, and note what happened the next day. Reread lessons 3, 4 and 10 as needed.
    Days 8 to 14Mechanics, tiny riskStart paper trading. Spot only, no leverage. Risk 1% per trade, use limit orders for entries, set a stop every time. Aim for quality, not quantity.
    Days 15 to 21Follow the plan, journal everythingUse your written plan from lesson 11. Journal each trade with a screenshot. Respect your daily loss limit.
    Days 22 to 30Review and honestyCalculate your results in R. Count how many trades broke the plan. Do the weekly review twice. Decide what to keep, drop and change.

    Keep a minimum target of around 30 trades under the same rules before judging anything. A handful of trades says almost nothing, because luck dominates small samples. Then, if you want more evidence, keep going another month.

    What "good" looks like after 30 days

    Note what is not on this list: a big profit. A great first month is boring.

    • You followed your risk rule on virtually every trade.
    • You always had a stop, and you never moved it farther away.
    • Your largest loss was close to your planned 1R, never a surprise.
    • You stayed within your daily loss limit.
    • Your journal is complete and honest.
    • You can explain why you took each trade.

    If your paper account is up, check whether it was skill or luck: did your wins come from planned setups, and would the results hold on another 30 trades? If it is down, that is information, not failure. Review the journal to see which rule you broke.

    If you decide to trade real money

    There is no rule that says you ever must. If you decide to, take these precautions:

    1. Only use money you can afford to lose entirely. Think of it as the price of learning, not as an investment you need back.
    2. Start small, small enough that a total loss would not affect your life. Keep the same 1% rule.
    3. Stay on spot with no leverage for a long time.
    4. Use a reputable, regulated exchange and turn on two-factor authentication. Never share recovery phrases.
    5. Expect it to feel different. Real money is more emotional. Cut your size even smaller at first.
    6. Learn the tax rules that apply where you live.
    7. Keep journaling. The journal never stops being useful.

    If you prefer not to trade actively at all, that is a valid choice. Our guide to dollar-cost averaging covers a lower-effort approach to owning crypto over time, with its own risks.

    What to remember

    • Spend 30 days: one week observing, then paper trade with 1% risk, then journal and review, aiming for around 30 trades under the same rules.
    • A good first month is judged by discipline (rules followed, stops kept, journal complete), not by profit.
    • If you ever use real money: only what you can lose, tiny size, spot first, a reputable exchange with 2FA.
    Try it: Start day 1 now: open the terminal, pick two pairs, and write your trend and zones on the daily chart. Put a reminder in your calendar for the day-8 paper trading start.

    Check your understanding

    Four quick questions. Wrong answers are fine: the explanation is where you learn.

    1. What should you do in the first week of the plan?

    The first week is for learning the tools and your market's behaviour with no pressure and no risk.

    2. About how many trades under the same rules give a more meaningful sample than just a handful?

    Small samples are dominated by luck. Around 30 trades gives a better, though still limited, picture.

    3. Which is the best sign of a successful first month?

    At this stage, discipline and process matter far more than a profit figure.

    4. If you decide to trade with real money, which approach fits this course?

    Tiny size, spot trading and strict risk rules keep a mistake survivable.
