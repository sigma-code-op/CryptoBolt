Context

What actually moves the Solana price

SOL follows the broad crypto market, but several of its drivers are specific to how Solana is used. These are the ones worth checking before you trade it.

On-chain trading activity

Low fees and fast blocks mean a lot of retail trading, including memecoin launches, happens on Solana decentralised exchanges. Surges in DEX volume bring transaction demand and attention to SOL; when memecoin activity cools, that demand can drop quickly. DEX volume is useful context that price alone does not show.

Staking and the inflation schedule

A large share of SOL is staked to secure the network, and stakers are paid from inflation. That inflation rate falls on a fixed schedule toward 1.5% a year, so new supply pressure shrinks over time. Staking is one reason a significant portion of SOL is not freely tradable.

Reliability and client diversity

Solana suffered several outages between 2021 and 2024, and each hurt sentiment while it lasted. Network stability has generally improved since, and independent validator software such as Firedancer is meant to reduce the risk that one bug stops the whole chain. Traders still treat reliability news as a price factor.

High beta and leverage

SOL usually moves further than BTC or ETH in the same direction, up or down. Combined with leveraged perpetuals, that makes sharp liquidation cascades more common than on larger coins.

Key idea: SOL depends partly on activity that can reverse fast, such as memecoin trading. A rally driven by on-chain volume can fade as quickly as that volume does, so size positions for the volatility rather than the last candle.

FAQ

Solana questions traders ask

Why are Solana transaction fees so low?

Solana is designed for high throughput, with very short block times and a fee model that charges a tiny base fee per transaction. In normal conditions a transfer costs a fraction of a cent, though fees can rise when users pay extra to get priority during busy periods.

Has Solana had outages?

Yes. The network halted several times between 2021 and 2024. Stability has generally improved since, but reliability remains something traders and validators watch closely.

Is SOL inflationary?

Yes, by design. Solana has no hard supply cap and pays stakers from new issuance. The inflation rate started near 8% and is scheduled to decline each year toward a long-term 1.5%.

Why does SOL move more than BTC?

SOL is a smaller market with a more speculative holder base, so the same flow of money moves its price further. That also means larger drawdowns, which is why leverage on SOL carries more risk than on Bitcoin.
