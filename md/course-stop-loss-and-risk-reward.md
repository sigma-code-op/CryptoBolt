# Stops and risk-reward: plan the exit before the entry

**By the end of this lesson:** Plan every trade with an entry, a stop and a target, and calculate whether the trade is worth taking before you place it.

A professional never enters a trade without three numbers already written down: where they get in, where they admit they are wrong, and where they will take profit. Beginners often have only the first.

## The stop: where your idea is proven wrong

A [stop-loss](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#stop-loss) is the price at which you close a losing trade. Good stops are not random distances like "5%". They go at a level where, if price reaches it, your reason for the trade no longer holds.

- **Long trade:** place the stop just below a recent low or a [support](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#support-and-resistance) level. If price breaks that, the buyers you were counting on have lost.
- **Short trade:** place it just above a recent high or [resistance](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#support-and-resistance).

Give a little breathing room. Price often pokes past an obvious level before turning, which is called a stop hunt or just noise. A stop exactly on a round number is easy to hit; a bit beyond is usually wiser. Our article on [round-number levels](https://cryptobolt.io/round-number-levels) explains why.

## The target and risk-reward

The target is where you plan to take profit. Compare it to your stop to get the [risk-reward ratio](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#risk-reward):

Traders call the risk amount **1R**. A trade that makes three times the risk is a **+3R** win, and a stopped-out trade is **−1R**. Counting results in R, not in dollars, lets you compare trades of any size. See [R-multiple](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#r-multiple).

## Why you do not need to win most trades

Here is the idea that surprises beginners: with a good reward-to-risk, you can lose more often than you win and still come out ahead. The break-even [win rate](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#win-rate) is:

| Risk-reward | Win rate needed just to break even |
| --- | --- |
| 1:1 | 50% |
| 1:2 | 33.3% |
| 1:3 | 25% |
| 1:0.5 (risking $2 to make $1) | 66.7% |

The last row is a warning: if you risk more than you stand to gain, you must be right most of the time just to break even. Many beginners do exactly this by taking quick small profits and giving big losses room to run.

**Expectancy** turns this into one number: the average result per trade, in R.

## The three-number checklist

1. Entry: at what price do I get in?
2. Stop: at what price am I wrong?
3. Target: where do I take profit, and is the reward at least about twice the risk?

If you cannot fill in all three, you are not ready to place the trade. Skip it. There will be another one tomorrow.

## Where does the stop actually go?

A good stop goes where your idea is proven wrong, not where a fixed percentage happens to land. If you buy because price bounced from a support area, a sensible stop sits just below that area. If price falls through it, the reason for the trade has gone. Then you work out position size from that distance, as in the previous lesson.

Three common mistakes to avoid: a stop so tight that normal noise hits it, a stop at an obvious round number where many others sit, and a stop that you move further away whenever price approaches it. The last one defeats the purpose of having a stop at all.

## Risk-reward and win rate work as a pair

Risk-reward compares what you might gain with what you might lose. If you risk $20 to make $60, the ratio is 1:3. What is less obvious is that the ratio only means something alongside how often you win.

| Risk-reward | Break-even win rate (before costs) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 : 1 | 50% |
| 1 : 2 | about 33% |
| 1 : 3 | 25% |
| 2 : 1 (risk 2 to make 1) | about 67% |

With a 1:3 setup you can lose three trades out of four and still break even. With a 2:1 setup you need to win two out of three just to stay level. Neither is automatically better. A strategy with a high win rate can use a small reward, and a strategy with a low win rate needs a large one. The trap is quoting a big ratio without checking whether the target is realistically reachable.

## Expectancy: the average result per trade

Expectancy combines both numbers. If you win 40% of the time, earn 2R on a win and lose 1R on a loss, then on average each trade returns (0.4 × 2) − (0.6 × 1) = 0.2R. That is a small edge. If you risk $20 per trade, you expect about $4 per trade before costs, and results will swing a lot around that average. Fees can easily eat a 0.2R edge, so check costs honestly.

## Setting a target without wishful thinking

Pick a target at a level where price has a reason to pause: a prior high, a round number, or an area where sellers appeared before. A target chosen only to make the ratio look pretty, such as "3x my risk because that sounds good", is a guess dressed as a plan. If the nearest sensible target gives you only 1:1, either accept a smaller size, wait for a better entry that improves the ratio, or pass on the trade.

Passing is a legitimate decision. Many good traders take only a few setups a week, because most of what they see does not offer a clear exit and a worthwhile reward at the same time.

## Scenario: a 1:2 plan written before the entry

Entry $100, stop $96, target $108. The risk is $4 and the reward is $8, a ratio of 1:2, so the plan breaks even at a 33.3% win rate. If you expect to win about four trades in ten, each trade carries an expectancy of about +0.2R before costs. The numbers are written down before the order is placed, so the exit does not get decided in the middle of a move.

### Common beginner mistakes here

- Moving the stop further away after the price approaches it.
- Taking profit early because a small gain feels safe, while still letting losses run to the stop.
- Setting a target that is far outside the recent range.

## What to remember

- Put the stop where your trade idea is proven wrong, and never move it farther away to dodge a loss.
- Risk-reward = reward ÷ risk. Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + R:R), so a 1:2 plan only needs to win about 34% of the time to break even.
- Every trade needs an entry, a stop and a target written down before you click.
