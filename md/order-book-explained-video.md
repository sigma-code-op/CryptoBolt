# How to read an order book

Bids, asks, the spread and slippage, animated.

## What you will learn

- The difference between bids (buyers) and asks (sellers)
- How the spread is the best ask minus the best bid
- Why a market order can fill at a worse average price than the one on screen

Read the transcript

Asks are sellers' orders waiting to sell, listed above the middle line. Bids are buyers' orders waiting to buy, listed below it. The spread is the best ask minus the best bid; in the example it is 0.01, and a tight spread usually means a liquid market. A market order to buy 10 units takes the cheapest asks first and then moves up the book to more expensive ones, so the average fill price ends up above the best ask. That gap is slippage. A very large bid, a so-called wall, can look like support, but it is only a promise and can be cancelled at any time. Remember: bids are buyers and asks are sellers, a wide spread or thin book means more slippage, and walls are hints, not guarantees.
