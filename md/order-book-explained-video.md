# How to read an order book

Bids, asks, the spread and slippage, animated.

## What you will learn

- The difference between bids (buyers) and asks (sellers)
- How the spread is the best ask minus the best bid
- Why a market order can fill at a worse average price than the one on screen

Read the transcript

Asks are sellers' orders waiting to sell, listed above the middle line. Bids are buyers' orders waiting to buy, listed below it. The spread is the best ask minus the best bid; in the example it is 0.01, and a tight spread usually means a liquid market. A market order to buy 10 units takes the cheapest asks first and then moves up the book to more expensive ones, so the average fill price ends up above the best ask. That gap is slippage. A very large bid, a so-called wall, can look like support, but it is only a promise and can be cancelled at any time. Remember: bids are buyers and asks are sellers, a wide spread or thin book means more slippage, and walls are hints, not guarantees. The numbers are illustrative and this is educational content, not financial advice.

## A worked example

Suppose the best bid is 100.00 and the best ask is 100.01, so the spread is 0.01. You send a market order to buy 10 units. The book has 4 units at 100.01, 3 at 100.02 and 3 at 100.03.

| Price level | Units filled | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100.01 | 4 | 400.04 |
| 100.02 | 3 | 300.06 |
| 100.03 | 3 | 300.09 |
| Total | 10 | 1,000.19 |

Your average fill is 100.019, a little above the best ask of 100.01. That difference is slippage. In a thin book, with fewer units at each level, the average fill would be worse. The numbers are illustrative.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a large resting order as guaranteed support or resistance. It can be cancelled at any moment.
- Sending a large market order into a thin book and getting a worse average price than expected.
- Looking only at the last traded price and ignoring the spread.
- Forgetting that the book changes constantly, so a screenshot is out of date almost immediately.

## Frequently asked questions

What is the spread?

The spread is the best ask minus the best bid. A tight spread usually means a liquid market with low trading costs.

What is slippage?

Slippage is the difference between the price you expected and the average price you actually got. It is larger when the order is big or the book is thin.

Is a big wall in the order book a reliable signal?

Not by itself. Walls can be real, but they can also be moved or cancelled. Treat them as a hint and check volume and price action too.

How do I avoid slippage?

Use limit orders when you can, trade liquid markets, and split large orders into smaller ones.
