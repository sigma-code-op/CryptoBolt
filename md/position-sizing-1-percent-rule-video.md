# Position sizing and the 1% rule

How to size a trade so one loss cannot do much damage.

## What you will learn

- How to turn a 1% risk limit into a position size
- The formula: position size = risk amount divided by stop distance
- Why compounded losses at 10% risk hurt far more than at 1%

Read the transcript

Start from what you are willing to lose. With a $1,000 account and a 1% risk limit, you risk $10 per trade. With an entry at $100 and a stop at $98, the stop distance is 2%, so the position size is $10 divided by 2%, which is $500. If the stop is hit you lose $10, which is 1% of the account. Why small risk matters: after ten losses in a row, risking 1% per trade leaves about $904 of the $1,000, while risking 10% per trade leaves about $349. Remember: decide the risk in dollars first, position size equals risk divided by stop distance, and smaller losses keep you in the game. The numbers are illustrative and this is educational content, not financial advice.

## A worked example

Account: $1,000. Risk limit: 1%, so $10 per trade. The same $10 risk gives a different position size depending on how far away the stop is.

| Stop distance | Position size (risk $10) | Loss if stopped out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1% | $1,000 | $10 |
| 2% | $500 | $10 |
| 5% | $200 | $10 |
| 10% | $100 | $10 |

The loss stays at $10 every time. Only the position size changes. Then see what a losing streak does at different risk levels, starting from $1,000:

| Risk per trade | After 10 losses in a row | Gain needed to get back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1% | about $904 | about 11% |
| 2% | about $817 | about 22% |
| 5% | about $599 | about 67% |
| 10% | about $349 | about 187% |

## Common mistakes

- Deciding the position size first and the stop afterwards. Start with the risk in dollars.
- Moving the stop further away to avoid being stopped out, which quietly raises the risk.
- Raising risk after a loss to win it back faster. This is how small losses turn into big ones.
- Ignoring fees and slippage, which make the real loss a little bigger than planned.

## Frequently asked questions

What is the 1% rule in trading?

It means risking no more than 1% of your account on a single trade. The risk is the amount you lose if your stop-loss is hit, not the size of the position.

How do I calculate position size?

Divide the amount you are willing to risk by the stop distance as a fraction of the entry price. Risk $10 with a 2% stop and the position is $500.

Is 1% the right number for everyone?

No. It is a common starting point. Many beginners use 1% or less, while others choose different levels. The point is to choose a limit in advance and stick to it.

Does position sizing work with leverage?

Yes. Leverage changes how much margin you need, not how much you lose at your stop. Size from the stop distance and keep the stop well inside liquidation.
