# Crypto Trading Glossary: Plain-English Terms | CryptoBolt

> Plain-English definitions of crypto trading and market-data terms: funding rate, open interest, liquidation price, ATR, order book depth, and more.

## A

AI hallucination AI Also: hallucinate

When a language model states something confidently that is false or made up, such as an invented price or a news item that never happened. Read more: [How CryptoBolt's AI research stays grounded in real data](https://cryptobolt.io/ai-research-grounded-data.html)

AI trading agent AI Also: trading agent

Software that uses AI to analyze markets and place trades by itself. Unlike a fixed-rule bot, it can adapt, which also means you have to trust its judgment and not only its code. Read more: [When AI Meets Crypto: The New Frontier of Autonomous Finance](https://cryptobolt.io/ai-meets-crypto.html)

APR (annual percentage rate) Basics Also: annual percentage rate

The yearly rate of return with no compounding. 5% APR on 100 tokens is 5 tokens after a year. Compare it with APY (annual percentage yield), which includes reinvested rewards. Read more: [Crypto staking: APR vs APY, where rewards come from and what can go wrong](https://cryptobolt.io/staking-apr-vs-apy.html) · [Staking rewards calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/crypto-staking-calculator.html)

APY (annual percentage yield) Basics Also: annual percentage yield

The yearly rate of return including compounding, so rewards earn rewards. It is equal to or higher than APR (annual percentage rate); the more often rewards compound the larger the gap, though at normal rates the gap is small. Read more: [Crypto staking: APR vs APY, where rewards come from and what can go wrong](https://cryptobolt.io/staking-apr-vs-apy.html) · [Staking rewards calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/crypto-staking-calculator.html)

ATR (Average True Range) Indicators & levels Also: average true range

A measure of how far price typically moves in a period, commonly averaged over 14 candles. It is used to set stop distances and position sizes that fit current Volatility. CryptoBolt uses it for trade levels. Read more: [How CryptoBolt's AI research stays grounded in real data](https://cryptobolt.io/ai-research-grounded-data.html)

Automated market maker (AMM) Basics Also: AMM

A decentralised exchange design where a formula, rather than buyers and sellers placing orders, sets the price. In the classic version the pool keeps the product of its two token balances constant, so each trade moves the price automatically. Read more: [Impermanent loss explained: why pool tokens can lag simply holding](https://cryptobolt.io/impermanent-loss-explained.html)

## B

Backtest Risk & performance

Running a trading rule over past data to see how it would have performed. It is useful for spotting a bad idea early, but a good backtest does not guarantee future results.

Bid-ask spread Market data Also: spread

The gap between the highest price a buyer will pay (best bid) and the lowest price a seller will accept (best ask). A tight spread usually signals a liquid market.

Bitcoin dominance Market data Also: BTC dominance

Bitcoin's share of the total crypto market capitalization, shown as a percentage. A rising number means Bitcoin is outweighing the rest of the market; a falling number means other coins are gaining share.

Bollinger Bands Indicators & levels Also: BB

A moving average with a band above and below it, set a couple of standard deviations away. The bands widen when Volatility rises and narrow when it falls.

Break-even price Risk & performance Also: breakeven

The price at which a trade makes neither profit nor loss once fees are counted. With the same percentage fee on the buy and the sell, it is the buy price divided by (1 minus the fee) squared. Read more: [Crypto ROI calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/crypto-roi-calculator.html)

## C

Candlestick Market data Also: candle, OHLC

A chart bar that summarizes one time period with four prices: open, high, low, and close. The thick body spans open to close; the thin lines above and below are the Wicks.

Compounding Basics Also: compound interest

Earning returns on earlier returns by reinvesting them. Compounding frequency matters less than the rate itself: at 10% APR the yearly APY is 10.00% while daily compounding gives about 10.52%. Read more: [Staking rewards calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/crypto-staking-calculator.html)

Cost basis / average buy price Basics Also: average entry price, average buy price

What you paid for a position, usually shown per coin as a weighted average of every purchase. It is the price at which the position breaks even before fees. Tax rules may require a specific lot-matching method instead of an average. Read more: [Average price calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/crypto-average-price-calculator.html)

Cross margin Futures & leverage

A setting where your whole futures wallet balance backs all your open positions. It makes liquidation less likely on any single position, but a bad one can drain the entire balance.

## D

Deterministic calculation AI Also: deterministic

A calculation that returns the same output every time for the same input. CryptoBolt computes entry, stop, and target levels this way with ATR (Average True Range) math instead of asking a language model for numbers. Read more: [How CryptoBolt's AI research stays grounded in real data](https://cryptobolt.io/ai-research-grounded-data.html)

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) Basics Also: DCA, cost averaging

Investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals, whatever the price. More coins are bought when the price is low and fewer when it is high, so the average cost lands between the highs and lows. It manages timing risk; it does not remove market risk. Read more: [Dollar-cost averaging in crypto: when it helps, when it does not](https://cryptobolt.io/dollar-cost-averaging-crypto.html) · [DCA calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/dca-calculator.html)

Drawdown Risk & performance Also: max drawdown

The drop from an account's peak value to a later low. Max drawdown is the biggest such drop over a period, and it shows how painful the ride was, not just where it ended. Read more: [How much can you realistically make day trading crypto?](https://cryptobolt.io/day-trading-crypto-realistic-returns.html)

## E

Expectancy Risk & performance Also: trading expectancy

The average result of a trade over many trades, usually in R-multiple. It equals win rate times average win minus loss rate times average loss. A positive value means the plan makes money on average; a short sample says little. Read more: [Risk-reward ratio and win rate: why a 40% win rate can still work](https://cryptobolt.io/risk-reward-ratio-and-win-rate.html)

## F

Fear & Greed index Sentiment & behavior Also: crypto fear and greed

A 0 to 100 score that summarizes crypto market mood from inputs like volatility, momentum, social activity, and dominance. Low means fear, high means greed. It describes mood now, not what comes next. Read more: [Reading the Fear & Greed index without overreacting to it](https://cryptobolt.io/fear-and-greed-index.html)

Fibonacci retracement Indicators & levels Also: fib levels, fib retracement

Horizontal reference levels at 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6% of a prior price swing, used to guess where a pullback might pause. They are shared reference points rather than predictions, and work best when they line up with other levels. Read more: [Fibonacci retracement calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/fibonacci-retracement-calculator.html)

FOMO Sentiment & behavior Also: fear of missing out

Fear of missing out: the urge to jump into a move because others are profiting. It tends to peak right after a big rise, which is when entries are usually worst. Read more: [$1000 in a day": why that headline is a trap, and why you clicked it](https://cryptobolt.io/1000-dollar-day-trap.html)

Funding rate Futures & leverage Also: funding, funding fee

A small periodic payment exchanged between long and short traders on a Perpetual futures contract. When it is positive, longs pay shorts; when it is negative, shorts pay longs. It nudges the contract price toward spot and hints at which side of the market is crowded. Read more: [Crypto funding rates: what the number on your futures screen means](https://cryptobolt.io/funding-rate-explained.html)

## G

Grounded AI AI Also: grounding

An AI answer built from live, checkable inputs such as current news, prices, and indicators rather than from the model's memory alone. Grounding reduces stale or invented claims. Read more: [How CryptoBolt's AI research stays grounded in real data](https://cryptobolt.io/ai-research-grounded-data.html)

## I

Impermanent loss Risk & performance Also: IL, divergence loss

The gap between what tokens are worth inside a Liquidity pool and what they would be worth if simply held, caused by the two prices moving apart. It shrinks if prices return and becomes permanent when you withdraw away from your entry price. Fees can offset it. Read more: [Impermanent loss explained: why pool tokens can lag simply holding](https://cryptobolt.io/impermanent-loss-explained.html) · [Impermanent loss calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/impermanent-loss-calculator.html)

Index price Futures & leverage

A blended spot price pulled from several exchanges. Futures platforms use it as a fair-value anchor when computing the Mark price.

Isolated margin Futures & leverage

A setting where only the margin you assign to one position is at risk. If that position is liquidated, the rest of your futures balance is untouched.

## L

Leverage Futures & leverage

Borrowing power that lets you open a position bigger than the money you put up. At 10x, $100 of your own money controls $1,000 of exposure. Gains and losses are both scaled up by the same factor. Read more: [Spot vs. perpetual futures: what actually changes](https://cryptobolt.io/spot-vs-futures.html)

Limit order Orders & execution

An instruction to buy or sell only at a price you choose or better. It may not fill if the market never reaches your price. Resting limit orders are what build up in the Order book.

Liquidation Futures & leverage Also: liquidated, forced close

The exchange forcibly closing your leveraged position because your Margin can no longer cover the losses. You lose the margin posted for that position. See Liquidation price. Read more: [Spot vs. perpetual futures: what actually changes](https://cryptobolt.io/spot-vs-futures.html)

Liquidation cascade Futures & leverage Also: long squeeze

A chain reaction where forced closes push the price further, which triggers more forced closes. Crowded leveraged positioning, often visible in Funding rate and Open interest, is what makes a cascade possible. Read more: [Crypto funding rates: what the number on your futures screen means](https://cryptobolt.io/funding-rate-explained.html)

Liquidation price Futures & leverage

The price at which your leveraged position would be forcibly closed. It depends on your entry price, Leverage, position direction, and the exchange's Maintenance margin rules, and it can shift when funding fees are paid. Read more: [Spot vs. perpetual futures: what actually changes](https://cryptobolt.io/spot-vs-futures.html) · [Liquidation price calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/liquidation-price-calculator.html)

Liquidity Market data

How easily you can buy or sell without moving the price. Deep, busy markets are liquid; thin ones are not, which means wider Bid-ask spread and more Slippage.

Liquidity pool Basics Also: LP, pool

A smart contract holding two or more tokens that traders swap against, used by decentralised exchanges instead of an order book. Providers who deposit tokens earn a share of the trading fees and take on Impermanent loss. Read more: [Impermanent loss explained: why pool tokens can lag simply holding](https://cryptobolt.io/impermanent-loss-explained.html)

Long and short Basics Also: long, short

Going long means betting the price will rise; you profit if it does. Going short means betting it will fall; you profit if it does. Shorting is mainly done with Perpetual futures rather than Spot trading. Read more: [Spot vs. perpetual futures: what actually changes](https://cryptobolt.io/spot-vs-futures.html)

## M

MACD Indicators & levels Also: moving average convergence divergence

A momentum indicator built from the difference between two exponential moving averages, plotted with a smoothed signal line. Crossovers and the histogram show momentum speeding up or slowing down.

Maintenance margin Futures & leverage

The minimum amount of equity a leveraged position needs to stay open. When your margin falls to this level, the exchange starts the Liquidation process. It is why real liquidation happens slightly before your margin hits zero. Read more: [Spot vs. perpetual futures: what actually changes](https://cryptobolt.io/spot-vs-futures.html)

Maker and taker Orders & execution Also: maker fee, taker fee

A maker adds a resting Limit order to the Order book; a taker removes liquidity by matching an existing order. Exchanges usually charge takers a higher fee than makers.

Margin Futures & leverage Also: collateral

The collateral you set aside to open and keep a leveraged position. Think of it as a deposit: if losses eat through it, the exchange closes the position. See Isolated margin and Cross margin. Read more: [Spot vs. perpetual futures: what actually changes](https://cryptobolt.io/spot-vs-futures.html)

Mark price Futures & leverage

The reference price an exchange uses to calculate unrealized profit and Liquidation on a futures position. It is usually based on the Index price rather than the last trade, so a single odd trade cannot trigger liquidations.

Market capitalization Market data Also: market cap

The total value of a coin: its current price multiplied by the number of coins in circulation. It is a rough size ranking, not a measure of how much money has flowed in.

Market order Orders & execution

An instruction to buy or sell right now at the best available price. It fills quickly but you do not control the exact price, so Slippage can occur.

Moving average (SMA and EMA) Indicators & levels Also: SMA, EMA, MA

The average price over the last N periods, redrawn on every new candle to smooth out noise. A simple moving average (SMA) weights all periods equally; an exponential one (EMA) weights recent periods more.

## O

Open interest Futures & leverage Also: OI

The total size of derivative contracts that are currently open and not yet closed. Rising open interest means new positions are being added; falling means positions are being closed. It is not the same as Volume. Read more: [Crypto funding rates: what the number on your futures screen means](https://cryptobolt.io/funding-rate-explained.html)

Order book Market data Also: bids and asks

A live list of resting buy orders (bids) and sell orders (asks), organized by price. It shows where traders are waiting to transact.

Order book depth Market data Also: depth, market depth

How much size is waiting at and around each price in the Order book. Large clusters, often at round numbers, can slow price down. See Support and resistance. Read more: [Why round numbers act like support and resistance](https://cryptobolt.io/round-number-levels.html)

## P

Paper trading Risk & performance Also: demo trading, simulated trading

Practicing with virtual money on live prices so mistakes cost nothing. It is a way to test a plan before risking anything real, though it cannot reproduce the emotions of real losses. Read more: [How much can you realistically make day trading crypto?](https://cryptobolt.io/day-trading-crypto-realistic-returns.html)

Perpetual futures Futures & leverage Also: perps, perpetual swaps, perpetual contracts

A derivative contract that follows an asset's price but never expires. You hold a position, not the coin itself. A recurring Funding rate payment between traders keeps the contract price close to the spot price. Read more: [Spot vs. perpetual futures: what actually changes](https://cryptobolt.io/spot-vs-futures.html) · [Crypto funding rates: what the number on your futures screen means](https://cryptobolt.io/funding-rate-explained.html)

Position sizing Risk & performance Also: risk per trade

Deciding how much to put into a trade, often by risking a small fixed slice of your account on each one. In the research summarized in our day-trading post, sizing mattered more than entry timing. Read more: [How much can you realistically make day trading crypto?](https://cryptobolt.io/day-trading-crypto-realistic-returns.html)

Profit factor Risk & performance

Total profit from winning trades divided by total loss from losing trades. Above 1 means the strategy made money overall; below 1 means it lost.

Prompt injection AI

An attack where text hidden in the data an AI reads tries to change its instructions. For an AI that trades or advises on markets, manipulated inputs can mean manipulated outputs. Read more: [When AI Meets Crypto: The New Frontier of Autonomous Finance](https://cryptobolt.io/ai-meets-crypto.html)

Proof of stake Basics Also: PoS

A way for a blockchain to agree on new blocks in which validators lock up the network token as collateral instead of spending energy on mining. Honest validators earn rewards and misbehaving ones can lose part of their stake. Read more: [Crypto staking: APR vs APY, where rewards come from and what can go wrong](https://cryptobolt.io/staking-apr-vs-apy.html)

Pump and dump Sentiment & behavior

A scheme where organizers buy a thinly traded coin, hype it so others pile in, then sell into the demand they created. Late buyers are left holding the loss. Read more: [$1000 in a day": why that headline is a trap, and why you clicked it](https://cryptobolt.io/1000-dollar-day-trap.html)

## R

R-multiple Risk & performance Also: R, 1R

A trade result measured in units of the amount risked. If you risk $50 and make $150 the trade is +3R; if the stop is hit it is -1R. Measuring in R makes trades of different sizes comparable and feeds Risk/reward ratio and Expectancy. Read more: [Risk-reward ratio and win rate: why a 40% win rate can still work](https://cryptobolt.io/risk-reward-ratio-and-win-rate.html) · [Risk-reward calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/risk-reward-calculator.html)

Risk/reward ratio Risk & performance Also: R:R

The potential profit of a trade compared with the potential loss. Risking $50 to make $150 is a 1:3 ratio. A good ratio does not help if the setup rarely works, so it is read together with Win rate.

Round-number level Indicators & levels Also: psychological level

A clean price such as $50,000 where traders tend to cluster orders because round figures are easy to remember. The reaction comes from the orders sitting there, not from the number itself. Read more: [Why round numbers act like support and resistance](https://cryptobolt.io/round-number-levels.html)

RSI (Relative Strength Index) Indicators & levels Also: relative strength index

A momentum gauge from 0 to 100 that compares recent gains to recent losses, commonly over 14 periods. Readings above about 70 are called overbought and below about 30 oversold, but in strong trends it can stay there for a long time.

Rug pull Sentiment & behavior

A scam where a project's creators attract money and then abandon it or drain the funds, leaving the token worthless.

## S

Short squeeze Futures & leverage

A sharp rise caused by short sellers being forced to buy back their positions, either by choice or by Liquidation. The buying itself pushes the price up, which squeezes more shorts.

Slashing Risk & performance Also: slashed

A penalty on proof-of-stake networks in which a validator loses part of its stake for serious faults such as signing two conflicting blocks. People who delegate tokens to that validator can share the loss. Read more: [Crypto staking: APR vs APY, where rewards come from and what can go wrong](https://cryptobolt.io/staking-apr-vs-apy.html)

Slippage Orders & execution

The gap between the price you expected and the price you actually got. It grows when you trade large sizes or when the Order book is thin.

Spot trading Basics Also: spot market

Buying or selling the actual coin at the current price. If you buy 0.1 BTC on the spot market, you own 0.1 BTC. There is no borrowing involved, so there is no liquidation. Compare Perpetual futures. Read more: [Spot vs. perpetual futures: what actually changes](https://cryptobolt.io/spot-vs-futures.html)

Stablecoin Basics Also: USDT, USDC

A crypto token designed to hold a steady value, usually one US dollar. Traders use stablecoins as a parking spot between trades and as the quote currency on many pairs, such as BTC/USDT.

Staking Basics Also: stake

Locking a proof-of-stake network token to help secure the network in return for rewards, usually paid in the same token. The dollar result depends on the token price, the reward rate, fees, lock-up periods and the risk of Slashing. Read more: [Crypto staking: APR vs APY, where rewards come from and what can go wrong](https://cryptobolt.io/staking-apr-vs-apy.html) · [Staking rewards calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/crypto-staking-calculator.html)

Stop-loss Orders & execution Also: stop

An order that closes a position automatically if price moves against you to a set level, capping the loss. On leveraged trades, a stop should sit well before the Liquidation price.

Support and resistance Indicators & levels Also: support, resistance

Support is a price area where buying has tended to appear and stop a fall. Resistance is an area where selling has tended to appear and stop a rise. Neither is a guarantee; levels break. Read more: [Why round numbers act like support and resistance](https://cryptobolt.io/round-number-levels.html)

Survivorship bias Sentiment & behavior

Judging a group by the winners you can see while ignoring the many who dropped out unseen. A screenshot of a big trading win says nothing about the accounts that quietly lost. Read more: [$1000 in a day": why that headline is a trap, and why you clicked it](https://cryptobolt.io/1000-dollar-day-trap.html)

## T

Take-profit Orders & execution Also: TP, target

An order that closes a position automatically once it reaches a profit target, locking in the gain without having to watch the screen.

Ticker Market data

A compact summary of a market: its current price, 24-hour change, and volume. The scrolling strip of prices on many sites is called a ticker or ticker ribbon.

Timeframe Market data Also: interval

The time period each Candlestick represents, such as 1 hour, 4 hours, or 1 day. Looking at several timeframes together shows whether short-term and long-term trends agree. Read more: [Why CryptoBolt doesn't predict crypto prices (and what to look at instead)](https://cryptobolt.io/why-we-dont-predict-crypto-prices.html)

Trading pair Basics Also: pair, symbol

Two assets quoted against each other, such as ETH/USDT. The first is what you are buying or selling; the second is what its price is measured in.

Trend Indicators & levels

The general direction price has been moving: higher highs and higher lows is an uptrend, lower highs and lower lows is a downtrend. A trend can be up on one Timeframe and down on another.

## V

Volatility Indicators & levels

How much and how fast price swings. High volatility means bigger moves in both directions, which is why it should change your position size and stop distance.

Volume Market data Also: 24h volume, trading volume

The amount of an asset traded during a period, such as 24 hours. Volume shows how much activity sits behind a price move; a breakout on thin volume is usually less convincing than one on heavy volume.

VWAP Indicators & levels Also: volume weighted average price

Volume-weighted average price: the average price over a period, weighted by how much traded at each price. Many traders treat it as a fair-value reference for the day.

## W

WebSocket stream Market data Also: live stream

A live connection where the server pushes updates the moment they happen, instead of the page asking again and again. It is how price tickers can update in real time.

Wick Market data Also: shadow

The thin line on a Candlestick showing how far price traveled beyond the open and close. A long wick means price went there but was pushed back. Read more: [Why round numbers act like support and resistance](https://cryptobolt.io/round-number-levels.html)

Win rate Risk & performance

The share of trades that end in profit. A high win rate alone does not mean a strategy makes money; the size of winners versus losers matters too. See Profit factor.
