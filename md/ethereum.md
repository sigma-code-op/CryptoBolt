The panel pairs the usual price numbers with a cross-rate, because Ethereum is judged against Bitcoin as much as against the dollar.

### Price and 24h change

The last traded ETH/USDT price on Binance spot and the rolling 24-hour move. ETH usually swings by a larger percentage than BTC on the same day, so a 3% move in ETH is often a quiet day for the wider market.

### ETH/BTC ratio

How many bitcoin one ether buys. If the ratio rises, ETH is outperforming Bitcoin even when both are falling in dollars; if it falls, ETH is lagging. Many traders read it as a gauge of appetite for risk beyond Bitcoin.

### 24h high, low and volume

The rolling-day extremes and the dollar volume on the ETH/USDT spot pair. ETH has the deepest liquidity among altcoins, so spreads are tight, but volume still thins out sharply at weekends.

### Funding rate

The payment between longs and shorts on the ETHUSDT [perpetual future](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#perpetual-futures). Persistently positive funding means the market leans long and longs are paying for it. Read the [funding rate guide](https://cryptobolt.io/funding-rate-explained) before treating it as a signal.

### Open interest

The total value of open ETH perpetual contracts. Because ETH is a leveraged favourite, [open interest](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#open-interest) can build quickly, and a sharp drop in it during a fall often marks forced closing rather than fresh selling.

Context

## What actually moves the Ethereum price

ETH responds to the same market forces as Bitcoin, plus several that come from how Ethereum itself works. These are the ones traders watch most.

### Staking and liquid supply

Since the Merge, new ETH is paid to validators who lock up their coins. A large share of supply sits in staking, which reduces what is available to trade. Changes in the validator entry and exit queues are watched as a sign of how confident holders are in staking yield.

### Fee burn and network activity

Under EIP-1559 part of every transaction fee is destroyed. When the network is busy, more ETH can be burned than is issued and supply shrinks; when activity is quiet, supply grows slowly. On-chain demand therefore feeds directly into ETH supply, which Bitcoin’s fixed schedule does not do.

### Layer 2s and upgrades

Most everyday activity has moved to layer-2 rollups that pay Ethereum for security and data. That can lower fees for users while shifting where value accrues, and scheduled protocol upgrades can change those economics. Upgrade dates are public, so the surprise is usually in how the market interprets them, not in the date.

### Stablecoins, DeFi and ETF demand

A large share of stablecoins and decentralised-finance applications run on Ethereum, which ties ETH demand to on-chain activity. US spot ETH funds, which began trading in July 2024, add a second demand channel that can be tracked through published flows.

History

## Key moments in Ethereum’s history

A short reference timeline of protocol milestones. These are fixed historical events, not predictions.

1. **July 2015**Ethereum mainnet launches with smart contracts.
2. **December 2020**The Beacon Chain starts, beginning the move to proof of stake.
3. **August 2021**EIP-1559 (the London upgrade) introduces the base-fee burn.
4. **September 2022**The Merge switches Ethereum from proof of work to proof of stake.
5. **March 2024**The Dencun upgrade cuts data costs for layer-2 rollups.
6. **July 2024**US spot Ether ETFs begin trading.
7. **May 2025**The Pectra upgrade raises the maximum validator balance to 2,048 ETH.

FAQ

## Ethereum questions traders ask

### Where does the live Ethereum price on this page come from?

It comes from Binance’s public market-data endpoints, requested directly by your browser and refreshed every ten seconds. The price is the ETH/USDT spot price, so it can differ slightly from other exchanges or from a US-dollar quote.

### What does the ETH/BTC ratio tell me?

It shows how many bitcoin one ether buys. A rising ratio means ETH is outperforming Bitcoin; a falling ratio means it is lagging, regardless of what both are doing in dollars.

### Is Ethereum’s supply fixed like Bitcoin’s?

No. Ethereum has no hard cap. New ETH is issued to validators, and part of every transaction fee is burned under EIP-1559, so supply can grow slowly or shrink depending on how busy the network is.

### What is staking, and how does it affect the ETH price?

Staking means locking ETH to help validate the network in return for rewards. Coins that are staked are not freely tradable, so a large staked share reduces liquid supply, while entry and exit queues show how many holders are joining or leaving.

### Why do layer-2 networks matter for ETH?

Layer-2 rollups such as Arbitrum, Optimism and Base process transactions cheaply and settle on Ethereum. They lower fees for users and increase Ethereum’s usage, but they also change how much fee revenue reaches the base layer, which traders debate.
