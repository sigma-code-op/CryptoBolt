# How much to risk: position sizing

**By the end of this lesson:** Work out the exact size of a trade from your account, your risk limit and your stop, instead of guessing.

New traders spend 95% of their effort on *when to buy* and almost none on *how much to buy*. Professionals do the opposite, because size is the one thing you fully control. You cannot control where price goes. You can control how much a wrong guess costs you.

## Risk is not the same as position size

These are different numbers, and mixing them up is the root of most blown accounts.

- **Position size** is how much you buy, for example $500 worth of a coin.
- **Risk** is how much you *lose if you are wrong*, meaning if price hits your stop.

A $500 position with a stop 2% away risks $10. A $500 position with a stop 20% away risks $100. Same size, ten times the risk. This is why you must know your stop *before* you decide the size.

## The 1% rule

A widely used guideline is to risk no more than **1% of your account on any single trade** (some use 0.5% to 2%). On a $1,000 account, that is $10 of risk per trade. It sounds tiny. That is the point.

## Why risk such a small slice?

Because losing streaks are normal, and losses are harder to recover from than they look. First, a streak: if you lose 10 trades in a row, which happens to everyone, risking 1% each leaves you down about 10%. Risking 10% each leaves you down about 65%.

| Risk per trade | Account after 10 losses in a row |
| --- | --- |
| 1% | about 90.4% left (down 9.6%) |
| 2% | about 81.7% left |
| 5% | about 59.9% left |
| 10% | about 34.9% left (down 65%) |

Second, the maths of recovery works against you:

| Loss from peak | Gain needed to get back |
| --- | --- |
| 10% | 11.1% |
| 25% | 33.3% |
| 50% | 100% |
| 75% | 300% |

A 50% loss needs a 100% gain to repair. Deep [drawdowns](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#drawdown) are where accounts die. Small, fixed risk keeps you in the game long enough to improve. You can test how likely a rough patch is with our [losing streak calculator](https://cryptobolt.io/losing-streak-calculator).

## Rules to take away

1. Decide your stop first, based on the chart. Never choose it based on how much you want to risk.
2. Then calculate size from the stop distance. A wider stop means a smaller position.
3. Do not increase size after wins out of excitement or after losses to "win it back".
4. If the calculated size is too small to be worth it, skip the trade. That is a feature, not a bug.

## What to remember

- Risk is the amount you lose if you are wrong, not the size of the position. Set the stop first, then calculate size.
- Size = (account × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop). Many traders cap risk at about 1% per trade.
- Big losses are disproportionately hard to recover from: a 50% loss needs a 100% gain.
