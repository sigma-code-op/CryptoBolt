# Reading a price chart: candles and timeframes

**By the end of this lesson:** Read any single candle at a glance and choose a sensible timeframe for what you want to do.

A price chart looks scary, but it is only a record of what the price did. The most common chart type uses [candlesticks](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#candlestick). Learn to read one candle and you can read the whole chart.

## One candle shows four prices

Each candle covers a fixed slice of time, for example one hour, and records four numbers:

- **Open:** the price at the start of that period.
- **High:** the highest price reached during it.
- **Low:** the lowest price reached during it.
- **Close:** the price at the end of the period.

The thick part is the **body**. It runs from open to close. The thin lines above and below are the **[wicks](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#wick)** (also called shadows). They show how far price went before being pushed back.

## What a candle can and cannot tell you

| What you see | What it describes |
| --- | --- |
| Long green body, tiny wicks | Buyers were in control for the whole period and price closed near the high. |
| Long red body, tiny wicks | Sellers were in control for the whole period. |
| Long upper wick | Price pushed up, then sellers drove it back down before the close. The move up was rejected. |
| Long lower wick | Price dropped, then buyers pushed it back up. The move down was rejected. |
| Small body, wicks both sides | A tug of war with no clear winner. Often called indecision. |

Notice the wording: these describe *what already happened*. A candle is a summary of the past, not a promise about the next one. Plenty of beginners memorise candle "patterns" with funny names and believe they predict the future. They do not, at least not reliably.

## Timeframes: the same market, different stories

A [timeframe](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#timeframe) is how much time each candle covers: 1 minute, 15 minutes, 1 hour, 4 hours, 1 day, 1 week. The same market can look like a crash on the 1-minute chart and a gentle pullback on the daily chart.

| Timeframe | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1m to 5m | Very short-term trading | Lots of noise, fast decisions, high costs |
| 15m to 1h | Day trading entries | Easy to overtrade |
| 4h to 1D | Seeing the bigger picture, swing trades | Slower, so patience matters |
| 1W | Long-term trend | Few signals |

For a beginner, **start on the 1-hour to 1-day charts**. They move slower, give you time to think, and cost less in fees because you trade less. Then zoom in to a smaller timeframe only to fine-tune an entry you already decided on.

## Volume: how many people took part

Under the candles you will usually see bars showing [volume](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#volume), the amount traded in that period. A big move on high volume means many participants agreed. A big move on thin volume is easier to reverse. Volume is context, not a signal on its own.

## Scenario: reading one candle

On the 1-hour chart a candle opens at $100, reaches a high of $104, a low of $98 and closes at $102. It is green because it closed above its open. The body ($100 to $102) shows where the hour started and ended. The upper wick ($102 to $104) shows buyers pushed higher but could not hold it, and the lower wick ($98 to $100) shows sellers pushed lower but were rejected.

### Common beginner mistakes here

- Memorising pattern names without checking trend and context.
- Comparing a 1-minute candle with a daily candle as if they meant the same thing.
- Reading a single candle as a signal.

## What to remember

- A candle records open, high, low and close for one time period; the body spans open to close and the wicks show the extremes.
- Candles describe what has already happened. They do not reliably predict what comes next.
- Beginners should start on 1-hour to 1-day charts; very small timeframes bring noise, stress and higher costs.
