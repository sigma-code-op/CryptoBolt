## A sensible order for a leveraged trade

1. **Position size** — decide the amount you are willing to lose, then let the stop-loss distance set the size.
2. **Risk-reward** — check that the target is worth the stop, and what win rate the plan needs.
3. **Liquidation price** — confirm your stop is hit well before the exchange would liquidate the position.
4. **Profit calculator** — check the reward after fees.
5. **Funding fee** — add the carrying cost if you plan to hold longer than a day or two.

## A sensible order for buying and holding

1. **DCA calculator** — see what regular buying would have cost compared with a lump sum.
2. **Average price** — combine your real purchases into one cost basis and a break-even price.
3. **ROI calculator** — include fees to see what a sale would actually return.
4. **Staking calculator** — project rewards, and remember the token price matters more than the rate.
5. **Impermanent loss** — if you provide liquidity, compare the pool with simply holding.

FAQ

## Free crypto trading calculators FAQ

### Are these calculators free?

Yes. They run in your browser, need no signup, and do not send your inputs anywhere.

### Are the results exact?

No. They are estimates using standard formulas. Exchanges add fees, tiered maintenance margin and funding, so check your exchange for the exact figure on a live position.

### Which calculator should I use first?

Start with position size: decide how much you are willing to lose, then check the liquidation price and fees for that position.

### Which calculator do I need to size a trade?

Use the position size calculator: enter your balance, the percentage you will risk, your entry and your stop-loss. It returns the size and the margin needed.

### Is there a calculator for buying a little at a time?

Yes. The DCA calculator models regular fixed-size buys and compares them with a lump sum, and the average price calculator combines your actual purchases.

### Can I calculate staking rewards and impermanent loss?

Yes. The staking calculator projects compounding rewards and converts APR to APY, and the impermanent loss calculator shows what a 50/50 liquidity pool costs versus holding.
