## What Fibonacci retracement levels are

After a strong move, price rarely continues in a straight line. It usually pulls back part of the way before the next leg. Fibonacci retracement levels are a set of horizontal reference lines that mark common pullback depths as a share of the prior move: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6%. The ratios come from the Fibonacci sequence (61.8% is the "golden ratio"; 38.2% is its complement), although 23.6% and 78.6% are derived from them and 50% is not a Fibonacci ratio at all but is included by convention. Traders use the levels to decide where a pullback might find buyers or sellers and where to place a stop or a target.

## How the levels are calculated

First pick a **swing low** and a **swing high** that define the move you are measuring. The difference is the range. For an uptrend, each retracement level is the high minus the range multiplied by the ratio; for a downtrend it is the low plus that product.

- **Uptrend retracement** = swing high − (range × ratio)
- **Downtrend retracement** = swing low + (range × ratio)
- **Uptrend extension** = swing low + (range × extension ratio)
- **Downtrend extension** = swing high − (range × extension ratio)

## Worked example

Bitcoin rises from $50,000 to $70,000, a range of $20,000. The 38.2% retracement is $70,000 − ($20,000 × 0.382) = $62,360. The 50% level is $60,000 and the 61.8% level is $57,640. If price falls back and stalls near one of these, traders call it a retracement holding. The extension levels project beyond the high: 127.2% is $75,440 and 161.8% is $82,360. Those are places where some traders might take profit if the uptrend resumes.

## Choosing the swing points

- Use **clear, obvious swings** that most people looking at the same chart would pick. A level only matters if enough participants watch it.
- Use the **wick extremes or the closing prices** consistently; mixing them shifts every level slightly.
- Measure from the **start of the move to its end**, on a timeframe that matches how long you plan to hold. Levels from a daily swing carry more weight than levels from a five-minute swing.

## Using retracements without over-trusting them

There is no proof that price respects Fibonacci levels more than chance would predict. What they do offer is a consistent, shared set of reference points, which is useful for planning a stop and target. The more evidence that lines up at one price, the more it deserves attention: a retracement level that coincides with a prior high or low, a round number such as [$60,000](https://cryptobolt.io/round-number-levels.html), a moving average or a high-volume area is a stronger area than a level standing alone. Treat levels as zones, not exact prices, and decide in advance where you would be wrong.

## Limitations

Different traders pick different swings, so different charts show different levels. Levels do not predict direction; they only describe where a move might pause. Strong trends can slice through every level, and ranging markets can ignore them. This calculator uses the standard ratios; some platforms add extras such as 88.6% or 141.4%.

## Fibonacci retracement calculator FAQ

### How do I calculate Fibonacci retracement levels?

Subtract the swing low from the swing high to get the range. In an uptrend each level is the high minus the range times the ratio (0.236, 0.382, 0.5, 0.618, 0.786); in a downtrend it is the low plus that amount.

### Which Fibonacci retracement level is most important?

The 61.8% and 38.2% levels are the most watched, with 50% close behind. None is reliable on its own; confluence with other levels or structure matters more.

### What are Fibonacci extension levels?

They project beyond the end of the move, using ratios such as 127.2%, 161.8% and 261.8%, to suggest where price might travel if the trend resumes. Traders often use them as profit targets.

### Do Fibonacci levels work in crypto?

They are widely used in crypto charts, and because many traders watch the same levels they can act as areas of attention. They are not predictive and should be combined with other evidence and a defined stop-loss.

### Should I measure from the wick or the candle body?

Either can work as long as you are consistent. Many traders use the wick extremes for the swing; others prefer closing prices to avoid single-candle spikes.

### Is the 50% level a Fibonacci number?

Not strictly. It is not derived from the sequence, but it is included in nearly every retracement tool because markets often pull back about half of a move.
