# Simple analysis: trend, support and resistance

**By the end of this lesson:** Identify the direction of the trend, mark the key price zones, and use them to plan an entry, a stop and a target.

Technical analysis means studying past prices to make better decisions. Done sensibly, it does not predict the future. It helps you find places where a trade has a clear stop and a good reward-to-risk. You only need three simple tools to start.

## Tool 1: the trend

A [trend](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#trend) is the general direction price is moving.

- **Uptrend:** a series of *higher highs and higher lows*. Each rally goes further than the last, and each dip stops higher.
- **Downtrend:** *lower highs and lower lows*.
- **Range (sideways):** price bounces between a ceiling and a floor without making progress.

The saying "the trend is your friend" is popular because trades in the direction of the larger trend tend to be easier. As a beginner, check the **daily chart first**. Is it going up, down or sideways? Then look at the 1-hour chart for your entry. Avoid fighting a strong trend because a coin "looks too high" or "looks too low".

## Tool 2: support and resistance zones

[Support](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#support-and-resistance) is a price area where falling prices have often stopped and bounced, because buyers stepped in. **Resistance** is an area where rising prices have often stalled, because sellers appeared. Find them by looking left on the chart for places where price turned several times.

- They are **zones**, not exact lines. Draw a band, not a hairline.
- The more times a zone has held, the more people watch it, but also the likelier it will eventually break.
- When support breaks, it often becomes resistance, and the reverse.
- [Round numbers](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#round-number-level) like $50,000 often act as zones. See [why](https://cryptobolt.io/round-number-levels).

## Tool 3: a moving average

A [moving average](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#moving-average) (MA) is the average closing price over the last N candles, redrawn on every new candle. It smooths out noise so the direction is easier to see. A common beginner choice is the 50-period MA: price above a rising 50-MA is a healthy uptrend; price below a falling one is a downtrend. It lags, meaning it shows what has already happened.

## Adding volume as context

A move through a zone on strong [volume](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#volume) is more meaningful than one on weak volume. A bounce off support on rising volume suggests real buyers. Treat volume as a clue, not a rule.

## The honest limits of analysis

- Every tool here describes the past. None predicts the future.
- Smart people look at the same chart and disagree. If someone says a pattern "always works", they are guessing or selling something.
- More indicators do not equal better decisions. Three clean tools beat fifteen cluttered ones.
- News, big sellers and sudden events can ignore every line you draw. That is exactly why you use a stop and size by risk.

Read [why we do not predict prices](https://cryptobolt.io/why-we-dont-predict-crypto-prices) for the longer version.

## Four questions before any entry

1. What is the trend on the daily chart?
2. Where are the nearest support and resistance zones?
3. Where is my stop (the price that proves me wrong), and how big does that make my position?
4. Is the reward at least about twice the risk?

## Reading trend without fooling yourself

A simple, testable definition of an uptrend is a series of higher highs and higher lows. A downtrend is lower highs and lower lows. Anything else, with price bouncing between two levels, is a range. Using a clear rule matters because the eye is very good at seeing trends in random noise.

Check yourself with this exercise: pick a chart, hide the right side, and decide whether the last 30 candles form a trend or a range using the rule above. Then check what happened. You will often find that what looked like a strong trend was a range, and that is a valuable thing to learn before you risk money on it.

## Levels are zones, not lines

Support and resistance are better thought of as areas where orders have been concentrated, not exact prices. If price turned around $100 twice, the reaction may have occurred anywhere between $99.50 and $100.60. Draw a band, not a hairline. Then plan your stop beyond the whole band, because price often pokes just through the line before reversing.

A level is stronger when it has been tested several times, when the reactions were sharp, and when it sits on a longer timeframe. A level on the 1-hour chart that lines up with one on the daily chart carries more weight than either alone. Round numbers, such as $100 or $60,000, also attract orders because people like to place them there. See the article on {{round-number-levels|round number levels}} for the detail.

## When a level breaks

When price closes clearly beyond a level and stays there, old resistance often becomes new support, and vice versa. This "role reversal" is common, but it is a tendency, not a law. A break that quickly falls back inside the range is called a failed breakout, and it can trap traders who bought the break. Many beginners enter on the first touch of a new high. A more careful approach is to wait for a close beyond the level and a retest that holds, accepting that you will sometimes miss part of a move.

## Turning this into a plan

1. Identify the trend on a higher timeframe using the higher highs and lows rule.
2. Mark the two or three zones that mattered most recently.
3. Define your idea in the form "if price holds above this zone, I buy, and I am wrong below it".
4. Check the distance to the next zone to see whether the reward justifies the risk.
5. If the trend and the zones disagree, or the picture is unclear, do nothing.

## Scenario: a range and what breaks it

Price has moved between $90 and $100 for two weeks and bounced off $90 three times. The $90 area is support because buyers have repeatedly stepped in there. If a candle closes at $88 on strong volume, the idea that buyers will defend $90 is wrong, and your plan should already say what you do then. Levels are places where decisions get made, not guarantees.

### Common beginner mistakes here

- Drawing a dozen levels on the chart until every price is near one.
- Treating a wick through a level as a break without waiting for a close.
- Entering only because price is at a level with no plan for being wrong.

## What to remember

- An uptrend is higher highs and higher lows; a downtrend is lower highs and lower lows; otherwise price is ranging.
- Support and resistance are zones where price has turned before. They give natural places for stops and targets.
- Indicators describe the past. Use a few simple tools, and rely on stops and sizing for the times they are wrong.
