# What trading is (and what it is not)

**By the end of this lesson:** Explain what trading is in one sentence, tell it apart from investing, and know honestly what to expect as a beginner.

Trading means buying or selling something, such as a coin, a share or a currency, with the plan of selling or buying it back later at a better price. That is the whole idea. Everything else in this course is about doing it without wrecking your account in the process.

## Trading versus investing

People mix these up, and it causes real damage. They are different activities with different rules.

|  | Investing | Trading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time horizon | Years | Minutes to weeks |
| Main idea | Own something that may grow in value, and wait | Profit from price moves, in either direction |
| How often you act | Rarely | Often |
| Biggest enemy | Panic selling and bad picks | Costs, leverage and emotions |
| Skill needed | Patience, diversification | Risk control, discipline, a repeatable process |

Neither is "better". But trading is harder than it looks, because on every trade you are competing against other people, some of whom are full-time professionals and automated programs.

## The honest picture

You will see ads showing someone turning a small account into a large one in a week. Those are marketing, not typical outcomes. Research on large sets of retail day-trading accounts keeps reaching the same conclusion: most people lose money once costs are counted, and only a small minority earn consistent profits. Our own articles on [realistic day-trading returns](https://cryptobolt.io/day-trading-crypto-realistic-returns) and the ["$1,000 in a day" headline trap](https://cryptobolt.io/1000-dollar-day-trap) go through why.

That is not meant to scare you off. It is meant to set the right target. If you protect your account while you learn, you give yourself years to improve. If you lose it all in the first month, you get no second chance.

## What you will be able to do by the end

- Read a price chart and understand what each candle says.
- Place the right type of order and know what it costs.
- Understand [leverage](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#leverage) and why it is the number one account-killer.
- Decide how much to risk on each trade, and where to get out before you get in.
- Spot the emotional traps that beat most beginners.
- Write a simple trading plan and practise it with fake money for 30 days.

## Three ground rules for the whole course

1. **Only ever risk money you can afford to lose completely.** Not rent money, not borrowed money, not savings you need.
2. **Practise with [paper trading](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#paper-trading) first.** It uses virtual money and real prices, so mistakes cost nothing.
3. **Nobody can predict prices.** Not us, not an influencer, not an AI. You will learn to manage risk instead of guessing the future. See [why we do not predict prices](https://cryptobolt.io/why-we-dont-predict-crypto-prices).

This course is education, not financial advice. Trading carries a high risk of loss.

## Scenario: what a small trade really looks like

Sam buys $200 of a coin at $50 and sells at $55. That is 4 coins, a $220 sale and a gross gain of $20. With a 0.1% fee on each side, fees are about $0.42, so the net profit is about $19.58. Notice what is missing: a signal, a prediction or a secret. A trade is a decision about price, size and exit, plus the cost of making it.

### Common beginner mistakes here

- Thinking trading is predicting. It is managing risk while you are wrong more often than you expect.
- Judging a trade by the outcome instead of by whether the plan was followed.
- Starting with money you cannot afford to lose.

## What to remember

- Trading is opening and closing positions to profit from price moves; investing is owning for the long term.
- Most retail traders lose money after costs. Your first goal is to protect your account while you learn.
- Use paper trading first, risk only money you can afford to lose, and never trust anyone who promises returns.
