How to read it

## What each Solana number tells you

SOL’s numbers read differently from Bitcoin’s because the coin is smaller, more volatile and more tied to activity on its own network.

### Price and 24h change

The last traded SOL/USDT price on Binance spot and the rolling 24-hour move. SOL regularly moves several percent in a day, so judge a move against its own recent range rather than against Bitcoin’s.

### SOL/BTC ratio

How much bitcoin one SOL buys. A rising ratio means Solana is outperforming Bitcoin, which usually happens when traders are taking more risk; a falling ratio means money is rotating back toward BTC.

### 24h high, low and volume

The rolling-day extremes and the dollar volume on the SOL/USDT spot pair. Volume here can swing widely with memecoin and on-chain trading waves, so compare today’s volume with its own recent average.

### Funding rate

The payment between longs and shorts on the SOLUSDT [perpetual future](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#perpetual-futures). Because SOL attracts leveraged momentum traders, funding can run hot in strong rallies. See the [funding rate guide](https://cryptobolt.io/funding-rate-explained) for how to read it.

### Open interest

The total value of open SOL perpetual contracts. Sharp rises in [open interest](https://cryptobolt.io/glossary#open-interest) during a rally show leverage building; a drop during a fall often marks forced closing.

Context

## What actually moves the Solana price

SOL follows the broad crypto market, but several of its drivers are specific to how Solana is used. These are the ones worth checking before you trade it.

### On-chain trading activity

Low fees and fast blocks mean a lot of retail trading, including memecoin launches, happens on Solana decentralised exchanges. Surges in DEX volume bring transaction demand and attention to SOL; when memecoin activity cools, that demand can drop quickly. DEX volume is useful context that price alone does not show.

### Staking and the inflation schedule

A large share of SOL is staked to secure the network, and stakers are paid from inflation. That inflation rate falls on a fixed schedule toward 1.5% a year, so new supply pressure shrinks over time. Staking is one reason a significant portion of SOL is not freely tradable.

### Reliability and client diversity

Solana suffered several outages between 2021 and 2024, and each hurt sentiment while it lasted. Network stability has generally improved since, and independent validator software such as Firedancer is meant to reduce the risk that one bug stops the whole chain. Traders still treat reliability news as a price factor.

### Beta to the wider market

SOL is a higher-beta asset: in strong markets it often rises by more than Bitcoin, and in weak ones it often falls by more. That cuts both ways for leveraged traders, which is why position size matters more here than on BTC.

History

## Key moments in Solana’s history

A short reference timeline of network milestones. These are fixed historical events, not predictions.

1. **March 2020**Solana mainnet beta launches.
2. **September 2021**A network overload stalls the chain for many hours, the first of several high-profile outages.
3. **February 2023**Another halt requires validators to coordinate a restart.
4. **February 2024**A further outage leads to renewed focus on client diversity and software upgrades.
5. **2024 onward**Firedancer, an independent validator client built by Jump Crypto, moves toward production use to reduce single-client risk.

FAQ

## Solana questions traders ask

### Where does the live Solana price on this page come from?

It comes from Binance’s public market-data endpoints, requested directly by your browser and refreshed every ten seconds. The price is the SOL/USDT spot price, so it can differ slightly from other exchanges or from a US-dollar quote.

### Why is SOL more volatile than Bitcoin?

SOL has a smaller market capitalisation and a more speculative trader base, and a lot of its demand comes from on-chain trading activity that can switch on and off quickly. Smaller, less liquid markets generally move further on the same amount of buying or selling.

### What is Proof of History?

Proof of History is a cryptographic clock that lets Solana validators agree on the order of transactions without waiting for each other, which is part of how the network reaches roughly 400-millisecond slots. It works alongside proof-of-stake consensus.

### How does Solana’s inflation affect the price?

Stakers are paid in newly issued SOL. The inflation rate is scheduled to fall by 15% each year toward a long-term 1.5%, so the supply added by issuance shrinks over time, though demand still decides what that means for price.

### Has Solana had outages, and do they still matter?

Yes. The network halted several times between 2021 and 2024. Reliability has improved since, but traders still watch for network problems because they can hit sentiment quickly.
