# Your first 30 days: a safe practice plan

**By the end of this lesson:** Follow a structured month of paper trading, judge your results honestly, and know exactly when and how to approach real money.

You have the ideas. Now you need reps. This final lesson turns everything into a 30-day plan you can follow with virtual money, and a clear test for when (and whether) to go further.

## The 30-day plan

| When | Focus | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1 to 7 | Watch and learn the tools | No trades yet. Open the [terminal](https://cryptobolt.io/app) daily, read the daily and 1-hour charts of 2 pairs, mark trend and support/resistance zones, and note what happened the next day. Reread lessons 3, 4 and 10 as needed. |
| Days 8 to 14 | Mechanics, tiny risk | Start [paper trading](https://cryptobolt.io/trade). Spot only, no leverage. Risk 1% per trade, use limit orders for entries, set a stop every time. Aim for quality, not quantity. |
| Days 15 to 21 | Follow the plan, journal everything | Use your written plan from lesson 11. Journal each trade with a screenshot. Respect your daily loss limit. |
| Days 22 to 30 | Review and honesty | Calculate your results in R. Count how many trades broke the plan. Do the weekly review twice. Decide what to keep, drop and change. |

Keep a minimum target of **around 30 trades** under the same rules before judging anything. A handful of trades says almost nothing, because luck dominates small samples. Then, if you want more evidence, keep going another month.

## What "good" looks like after 30 days

Note what is *not* on this list: a big profit. A great first month is boring.

- You followed your risk rule on virtually every trade.
- You always had a stop, and you never moved it farther away.
- Your largest loss was close to your planned 1R, never a surprise.
- You stayed within your daily loss limit.
- Your journal is complete and honest.
- You can explain why you took each trade.

If your paper account is up, check whether it was skill or luck: did your wins come from planned setups, and would the results hold on another 30 trades? If it is down, that is information, not failure. Review the journal to see which rule you broke.

## Graduation checklist

Tick these honestly. Your ticks are saved in this browser.

- I followed my risk rule (about 1% per trade) on every paper trade.
- I used a stop on every trade and never moved it farther away.
- My biggest loss was close to the 1R I planned, with no surprises.
- I stayed inside my daily loss limit.
- I wrote a journal entry, with a screenshot, for every trade.
- I made at least 30 paper trades under the same rules.
- I can explain leverage and liquidation to someone else in my own words.
- I know what I can afford to lose, and I will never risk money I need.

## If you decide to trade real money

There is no rule that says you ever must. If you decide to, take these precautions:

1. **Only use money you can afford to lose entirely.** Think of it as the price of learning, not as an investment you need back.
2. **Start small**, small enough that a total loss would not affect your life. Keep the same 1% rule.
3. **Stay on spot with no leverage** for a long time.
4. **Use a reputable, regulated exchange** and turn on two-factor authentication. Never share recovery phrases.
5. **Expect it to feel different.** Real money is more emotional. Cut your size even smaller at first.
6. **Learn the tax rules** that apply where you live.
7. **Keep journaling.** The journal never stops being useful.

If you prefer not to trade actively at all, that is a valid choice. Our guide to [dollar-cost averaging](https://cryptobolt.io/dollar-cost-averaging-crypto) covers a lower-effort approach to owning crypto over time, with its own risks.

## A week-by-week plan for your first month

**Week 1: observe.** Open the terminal each day for fifteen minutes. Watch how the order book behaves, how candles form, and how price reacts near round numbers. Do not trade yet. Write down three things you noticed each day.

**Week 2: practise the mechanics.** Place small paper trades using one market and one setup. The goal is not profit. It is to get used to placing entries, stops and targets quickly and correctly, and to recording every trade in your journal.

**Week 3: practise the rules.** Add your risk limits: a fixed risk per trade, a daily loss limit and a maximum number of trades. Count how often you break your own rules. That number is more telling than your profit.

**Week 4: review and decide.** Run a full review of the month. Compute your average result in R, how often you followed the plan, and your costs. Decide honestly whether you are ready to try a very small real position, or whether you need another month.

## A readiness checklist

Move to real money only when you can say yes to most of these:

- I followed my plan on at least 80% of my paper trades.
- I know my risk per trade in dollars before I enter.
- I understand the liquidation price on any leveraged position I take.
- I kept a journal for the whole month and reviewed it weekly.
- I can explain what I would do if a trade went wrong, without improvising.
- The amount I would use is money I could lose without changing my life.

If you cannot tick enough boxes, nothing is lost. Another month of practice costs nothing, and you will be more prepared than most people who start.

## Your first real trades

When you do move to real money, shrink everything. Use a much smaller risk than you used in paper trading, maybe a quarter of it, because real money feels different. The aim of the first month is not profit, it is to see how you behave when the loss is real, and whether your rules survive. Many people find that rules which were easy on paper become hard with real money. That is normal, and it is the reason for starting small.

Keep the journal going, and keep your daily loss limit. If you break your rules, go back to paper trading for a week, without shame. It is a sign that your process needs work, not that you have failed.

## Scenario: a sample 30-day scorecard

After 30 days on paper, a beginner has 22 logged trades, 9 wins averaging +1.6R and 13 losses of -1R. The result is (9 x 1.6 - 13) / 22, about +0.06R per trade. That is not proof of anything: 22 trades is a tiny sample. The real result of the month is that every trade has a plan, a size and a note, which is the habit you will take to real money.

### Common beginner mistakes here

- Going live after a week of paper profits.
- Changing the strategy every few days so nothing is ever tested.
- Skipping the journal because the paper money is not real.

## What to remember

- Spend 30 days: one week observing, then paper trade with 1% risk, then journal and review, aiming for around 30 trades under the same rules.
- A good first month is judged by discipline (rules followed, stops kept, journal complete), not by profit.
- If you ever use real money: only what you can lose, tiny size, spot first, a reputable exchange with 2FA.
