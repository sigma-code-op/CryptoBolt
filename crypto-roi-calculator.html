    What ROI means

    Return on investment (ROI) is the profit or loss on a trade as a percentage of the money you put in. Unlike a raw dollar profit, it lets you compare a $50 trade with a $5,000 trade on equal terms. For a crypto purchase, the honest version includes fees on both the buy and the sell, because they come straight out of your return.

    The formulas

    • Coins bought = amount invested × (1 − fee) ÷ buy price (the buy fee is taken out of what you spend)
    • Proceeds = coins × sell price × (1 − fee) (the sell fee is taken out of what you receive)
    • Net profit = proceeds − amount invested
    • ROI = net profit ÷ amount invested × 100
    • Break-even sell price = buy price ÷ (1 − fee)2

    Worked example

    You invest $1,000 in a coin at $2,000 with a 0.1% fee each way and sell at $2,600. The buy fee leaves $999 to buy 0.4995 coins. Selling them at $2,600 returns $1,298.70, and the sell fee takes about $1.30, leaving about $1,297.40. Net profit is about $297.40, an ROI of 29.74%, a little below the 30% price move because total fees are about $2.30. Break-even is about $2,004.01 — the price must rise roughly 0.2% before the trade is profitable at all.

    Why fees matter more on small moves

    Fees are a fixed percentage of the trade, so they eat a larger share of a small profit. At 0.1% each way, a 1% price move leaves about 0.8% after fees — a fifth of the gain is gone. At 0.5% each way (common on simple buy-and-sell apps that add a spread), the break-even move is about 1%. If you trade often, the fee rate is one of the few variables you control completely.

    Recovering from a loss

    FAQ

    Crypto ROI calculator FAQ

    How do I calculate ROI on a crypto trade?

    Subtract the amount you invested from what you got back after fees, divide by the amount invested, and multiply by 100. This calculator also includes the fee on the buy and on the sell.

    What is the break-even price of a crypto trade?

    It is the sell price at which fees are fully covered and profit is zero. With the same fee on both sides it equals the buy price divided by (1 − fee) squared.

    Is ROI the same as profit?

    No. Profit is the amount of money gained; ROI is that profit as a percentage of the amount invested. A $100 profit is a 10% ROI on $1,000 and a 1% ROI on $10,000.

    Does this include taxes?

    No. Tax rules vary by country and can change your net return. Check local rules or ask a tax professional.

    Why is my ROI lower than the price change?

    Because fees are charged on the way in and on the way out. On a 30% price rise with 0.1% fees each way, ROI is about 29.7%.

    Can I use this for leveraged trades?

    No. This is for spot trades. For leveraged futures use the futures profit calculator, which handles margin, leverage and return on equity.
