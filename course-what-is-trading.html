    What trading is (and what it is not)

    By the end of this lesson: Explain what trading is in one sentence, tell it apart from investing, and know honestly what to expect as a beginner.

    Trading means buying or selling something, such as a coin, a share or a currency, with the plan of selling or buying it back later at a better price. That is the whole idea. Everything else in this course is about doing it without wrecking your account in the process.

    Definition: A trade has two sides: an entry (you open the position) and an exit (you close it). The difference between the two prices, minus costs, is your result.

    Trading versus investing

    People mix these up, and it causes real damage. They are different activities with different rules.

    InvestingTrading
    Time horizonYearsMinutes to weeks
    Main ideaOwn something that may grow in value, and waitProfit from price moves, in either direction
    How often you actRarelyOften
    Biggest enemyPanic selling and bad picksCosts, leverage and emotions
    Skill neededPatience, diversificationRisk control, discipline, a repeatable process

    Neither is "better". But trading is harder than it looks, because on every trade you are competing against other people, some of whom are full-time professionals and automated programs.

    The honest picture

    You will see ads showing someone turning a small account into a large one in a week. Those are marketing, not typical outcomes. Research on large sets of retail day-trading accounts keeps reaching the same conclusion: most people lose money once costs are counted, and only a small minority earn consistent profits. Our own articles on realistic day-trading returns and the "$1,000 in a day" headline trap go through why.

    Key idea: For a beginner, the first goal is not to make money. It is to not lose much while you learn. Survive first, then get good, then think about profit.

    That is not meant to scare you off. It is meant to set the right target. If you protect your account while you learn, you give yourself years to improve. If you lose it all in the first month, you get no second chance.

    What you will be able to do by the end

    • Read a price chart and understand what each candle says.
    • Place the right type of order and know what it costs.
    • Understand leverage and why it is the number one account-killer.
    • Decide how much to risk on each trade, and where to get out before you get in.
    • Spot the emotional traps that beat most beginners.
    • Write a simple trading plan and practise it with fake money for 30 days.

    Three ground rules for the whole course

    1. Only ever risk money you can afford to lose completely. Not rent money, not borrowed money, not savings you need.
    2. Practise with paper trading first. It uses virtual money and real prices, so mistakes cost nothing.
    3. Nobody can predict prices. Not us, not an influencer, not an AI. You will learn to manage risk instead of guessing the future. See why we do not predict prices.

    This course is education, not financial advice. Trading carries a high risk of loss.

    What to remember

    • Trading is opening and closing positions to profit from price moves; investing is owning for the long term.
    • Most retail traders lose money after costs. Your first goal is to protect your account while you learn.
    • Use paper trading first, risk only money you can afford to lose, and never trust anyone who promises returns.
    Try it: Open the paper trading page and just look around for five minutes. You do not need to place a trade yet. Find where your virtual balance is shown, and notice which pairs you can choose.

    Check your understanding

    Four quick questions. Wrong answers are fine: the explanation is where you learn.

    1. Which statement best describes trading?

    Trading is about entering and exiting positions to profit from price changes. There are no guarantees, and the time frame is usually shorter than investing.

    2. What should a beginner's first goal be?

    Survival comes first. Traders who protect their accounts early get time to improve; those who blow up early do not.

    3. Why is paper trading useful at the start?

    Paper trading uses real prices with virtual money, so you can practise the mechanics and your habits without risking savings. It does not guarantee later results.

    4. An ad says: 'Turn $100 into $10,000 in a week, guaranteed.' What is the right reaction?

    Guaranteed returns are the classic sign of a scam or of misleading marketing. Screenshots can be faked, and real traders never promise outcomes.
