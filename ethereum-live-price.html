Context

What actually moves the Ethereum price

ETH responds to the same market forces as Bitcoin, plus several that come from how Ethereum itself works. These are the ones traders watch most.

Staking and liquid supply

Since the Merge, new ETH is paid to validators who lock up their coins. A large share of supply sits in staking, which reduces what is available to trade. Changes in the validator entry and exit queues are watched as a sign of how confident holders are in staking yield.

Fee burn and network activity

Under EIP-1559 part of every transaction fee is destroyed. When the network is busy, more ETH can be burned than is issued and supply shrinks; when activity is quiet, supply grows slowly. On-chain demand therefore feeds directly into ETH supply, which Bitcoin's fixed schedule does not do.

Layer 2s and upgrades

Most everyday activity has moved to rollups that pay Ethereum for security. Upgrades such as Dencun in March 2024 made that cheaper for users. The open debate is whether cheaper layer 2s add value to ETH through more usage or take value away by collecting fees themselves.

ETH/BTC and DeFi demand

ETH is the main collateral in decentralised finance and a base asset for many stablecoin and token markets. A rising ETH/BTC ratio usually points to appetite shifting toward smart-contract assets; a falling one means Bitcoin is leading. US spot ETH ETFs, trading since July 2024, add a second source of flow data.

Key idea: Look at ETH against BTC, not only against USDT. If ETH is flat in dollars while Bitcoin rallies, ETH is losing ground even though its dollar price has not fallen.

FAQ

Ethereum questions traders ask

What does gas mean on Ethereum, and why do fees change?

Gas measures the computing work a transaction needs, and fees are quoted in gwei per unit of gas. Fees rise when many people compete for limited block space and fall when the network is quiet. Much routine activity now happens on layer 2s, where fees are usually lower.

Is Ethereum deflationary?

Not by design. ETH has no hard supply cap. New ETH is issued to stakers while a portion of every fee is burned, so net supply can shrink in busy periods and grow slowly in quiet ones.

What was the Merge?

The Merge was Ethereum's switch from proof of work mining to proof of stake in September 2022. It ended mining on Ethereum and cut the issuance of new ETH substantially.

Why does ETH often move more than BTC?

ETH is a smaller market than Bitcoin and is treated as a higher-risk, higher-beta asset, so it tends to rise and fall by larger percentages in the same direction. The ETH/BTC ratio shows that gap directly.
